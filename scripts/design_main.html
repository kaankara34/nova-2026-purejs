Architecture begins

Designed from the

A residence is shaped long before its finishes are selected. It begins with proportion, orientation, light and the relationship between one space and the next. At Nova, these fundamentals establish the framework from which every architectural decision develops.

Plans are developed around the way spaces are inhabited — balancing openness with privacy, daylight with shelter, and architectural clarity with the practical rhythms of everyday life.

Architecture

A Considered
Architecture

Architecture at Nova is approached as a study in proportion, sequence and permanence. Rather than relying on visual excess, each building is developed through disciplined geometry, carefully resolved circulation and a measured relationship between interior and exterior space.

Façades are composed to respond to scale, orientation and context. Inside, layouts prioritise usable dimensions, clear movement and natural light. The objective is not novelty for its own sake, but architecture capable of remaining relevant long after its completion.

Space & Proportion

Designed around how space is lived.

Residential planning begins with the relationship between rooms rather than their individual dimensions alone. Sightlines, circulation paths, furniture zones, thresholds and transitions are considered collectively so that the plan reads as a coherent whole.

Living spaces are given visual continuity while quieter areas retain privacy. Structural grids, service zones and architectural planning are coordinated from the outset to minimise unnecessary compromise in the finished residence.

The result is architecture that feels intuitive rather than prescribed.

Light

Light as an architectural material.

Controlled openness

Natural light is treated as part of the architecture itself. Window proportions, façade openings, room depths and orientation are developed together to create interiors that change subtly throughout the day.

Rather than maximising glazing indiscriminately, openings are considered according to view, privacy, solar exposure and spatial character. After dark, integrated lighting at 2700–3000K continues the same intent: concealed coves, joinery reveals and directional detail lighting, calibrated so that timber, stone and bronze retain their warmth.

A well-lit room should never feel overexposed.

Materiality

Materials chosen
to age with dignity.

Material selection is guided by permanence rather than novelty. Oak and walnut, bookmatched stone, aged bronze, brushed brass, leather and smoked glass are considered not only for their appearance at completion, but for how they will perform, patinate and acquire character across decades of use.

Grain direction, vein matching, junctions, tolerances and the transitions between materials receive the same attention as the materials themselves. A limited palette, applied with precision, reads as considerably richer than an assembled variety.

Quality is often most visible where two materials meet.

Interiors

Quiet interiors,
precisely resolved.

Nova interiors are conceived as an extension of the architecture rather than a separate decorative layer. Wall panelling, bespoke joinery, lighting, material transitions and service interfaces are coordinated so that visual continuity is preserved throughout the residence.

The palette is deliberately warm and deliberately narrow — timber, stone, bronze and leather — allowing proportion, daylight, texture and craftsmanship to establish character. Furniture, art and collected objects are placed as part of the composition rather than added to it.

A home should reveal its quality gradually — through the way a door closes, the alignment of a joint, the depth of a reveal or the texture of a surface.

Detail

Precision is most
visible at the edges.

Shadow gaps, thresholds, joinery reveals, panel junctions, ironmongery, integrated vents and concealed services are resolved as part of a single architectural language. These details are deliberately quiet, but collectively they determine how complete a residence feels in daily use.

Landscape

Architecture does not end at the façade.

Landscape is considered as part of the spatial composition of the residence. Terraces, courtyards, arrival sequences, boundaries and exterior living areas are coordinated with the architecture, so that stone paving, architectural hedging and integrated seating continue the language of the interior beyond the building envelope.

Sculptural trees, layered evergreen planting and restrained water elements are used to establish privacy, seasonal variation and a sense of maturity — without ornamental excess.

The relationship between architecture and landscape should feel established rather than applied.

Context

Designed for its place.

Residential architecture does not exist independently of its surroundings. Scale, orientation, neighbouring buildings, street character, mature vegetation and long-distance views all influence the architectural response.

Within Istanbul's established residential districts — Bağdat Caddesi among them — this requires particular sensitivity. New architecture must address contemporary expectations while maintaining a measured relationship with the urban fabric around it.

At Nova, context is treated as a design parameter rather than a backdrop.

The design process

From First Line
to Final Detail

  1. 01

    Context

    Site, orientation, planning constraints and surrounding urban conditions establish the first parameters.

  2. 02

    Proportion

    Structural geometry, spatial hierarchy and circulation are developed as a unified plan.

  3. 03

    Material

    Material systems are selected according to performance, tactility and architectural coherence — timber species and grain, stone selection and vein matching, metal finish and patina.

  4. 04

    Coordination

    Architecture, structure, façade, interiors and building services are resolved together before execution.

  5. 05

    Detail

    Critical junctions and interfaces are refined through drawings, samples and on-site review.

Design & execution

A design is only as precise as its execution.

Architectural intent depends on control during construction. Dimensions, interfaces, substrates, tolerances and material junctions are therefore reviewed not as isolated finishing items, but as part of the complete design.

Mock-ups, samples and site coordination are used where necessary to verify material behaviour, colour, texture, grain matching and junction quality before final execution.

The objective is simple: what is resolved in the drawings must remain resolved in the built work.
