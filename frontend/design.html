Design at Nova

Architecture begins with
proportion.

Planned from the
inside out.

A residence starts with its plan. Proportion, orientation, daylight and the relationship between rooms are established before finishes are considered. Those first decisions determine how the home works and set the direction for everything that follows.

Daily routines inform the layout. Living areas call for openness, private rooms for separation, and circulation for clarity. When the plan is right, none of this needs explaining.

Architecture

Architecture with
reason.

Nova develops architecture through clear planning and measured proportions. Structure, circulation, façade and interior space are resolved together, allowing the building to operate as a coherent whole.

The façade responds to scale, orientation, privacy and the character of its setting. Inside, the emphasis is on useful room dimensions, direct circulation and daylight. The objective is a home that remains convincing long after completion.

Space & Proportion

Proportioned for the way rooms are used.

Room size is only one measure of a plan. Sightlines, circulation, furniture placement, thresholds and transitions are studied together, because the value of each room depends on how it relates to the next.

Living spaces remain open where useful, while bedrooms and quieter areas keep their privacy. Structure, service zones and the architectural plan are coordinated early, before technical necessities begin to dictate the interior.

The result is a plan that is clear from the moment you enter.

Light

Light is part of the plan.

Controlled openness

Daylight shifts through a room over the course of the day. Window dimensions, orientation, room depth and façade openings are therefore assessed as one composition.

More glass is not automatically better. Views, privacy, solar exposure and thermal comfort determine where openings belong. After dark, concealed, joinery and task lighting provide distinct layers without flattening the room.

Light should serve the room, not dominate it.

Materiality

Materials with
substance.

Materials are judged by appearance, touch and performance in use. Stone, timber, metal, leather and glass each weather differently; their behaviour over time matters as much as how they look on completion day.

Timber grain, stone veining, joint widths and transitions are set out deliberately. A restrained group of compatible materials gives an interior more authority than a catalogue of finishes competing for notice.

The junction between two materials reveals the standard of the work.

Material study

Five surfaces,
examined closely.

Chosen according to the project brief and client preference, these five surfaces form part of Nova's material language. Rotate each sample to review its origin, finish and behaviour over time.

3D — drag to inspect

01 / 05  ·  Walnut

  1. 01

    Walnut

    Origin
    American black walnut — Juglans nigra — sourced from the eastern United States and southeastern Canada.
    Cut
    Plain-sliced leaves show a broad cathedral grain; quarter-cut leaves produce a quieter, more linear figure.
    Surface
    Sequence-matched, hand-sanded and finished in natural oil or low-sheen lacquer without obscuring the grain.
    Used for
    Wall panelling, wardrobe fronts, door leaves and integrated joinery.
    How it ages
    Daylight gradually mellows the deep brown tone towards a warmer amber; minor marks can be locally refinished.

  2. 02

    Bookmatched stone

    Origin
    Calacatta Oro or Arabescato marble from the Apuan Alps in Tuscany, selected as numbered consecutive slabs.
    Pattern
    Mirrored adjoining slabs carry the natural veining continuously across the joint.
    Surface
    Honed for a quiet, tactile finish or polished where greater depth and contrast are required.
    Used for
    Entrance walls, bathroom surfaces, feature panels and worktops.
    How it ages
    Sealed surfaces retain their tone; acid-sensitive marble requires appropriate care.

  3. 03

    Aged bronze

    Origin
    Solid bronze and solid-brass components made by specialist foundries and metal workshops in northern Italy.
    Composition
    Cast bronze forms sculptural pieces; solid brass is reserved for precision hardware.
    Surface
    Hand-brushed, chemically patinated, then protected with wax or clear lacquer.
    Used for
    Ironmongery, joinery pulls, reveal trims, lighting details and balustrades.
    How it ages
    Unlacquered patina deepens; handled edges gradually become brighter.

  4. 04

    Leather

    Origin
    Full-grain bovine hides vegetable-tanned by specialist tanneries in Tuscany, between Florence and Pisa.
    Grain
    Natural grain and subtle hide variations remain visible; the surface is not embossed.
    Surface
    Aniline drum-dyed and oil-and-wax nourished, with burnished edges and tonal saddle stitching.
    Used for
    Seating, headboards, wardrobe interiors, drawer linings and handrail wraps.
    How it ages
    It softens, deepens in colour and develops a distinct patina through use.

  5. 05

    Smoked glass

    Origin
    Body-tinted float glass from leading Belgian, German or Turkish architectural glass manufacturers.
    Composition
    Mineral colourants added to the batch create a grey or bronze body tint.
    Surface
    Edge-polished, then tempered or laminated where safety and loading require it.
    Used for
    Cabinet fronts, room dividers, shower screens, bars and shelving.
    How it ages
    The integral tint remains stable and cannot peel from the surface.

Interiors

Warmth without
excess.

The interior is developed as part of the architecture. Wall panelling, built-in joinery, lighting, material transitions and service details are coordinated from the outset, rather than added as separate layers after the plan is complete.

The palette is warm and restrained. Timber, stone, metal, leather and textiles give the rooms depth without visual noise. Furniture, artwork and personal objects can then occupy the space without competing with its architecture.

The standard becomes evident in use — the closing of a door, the alignment of two panels, the weight of a handle, the depth of a reveal and the touch of a surface.

Detail

Precision is visible at
the edges.

Shadow gaps, thresholds, joinery lines, metalwork, integrated vents and concealed services are resolved before finishes begin. Their purpose is not to attract notice, but to remove the visual interruptions that weaken a room.

Landscape

The plan continues
outside.

Landscape forms part of the residential plan. Entrances, terraces, courtyards, planting and outdoor seating are developed with the building, giving the route between interior and exterior a clear purpose.

Existing trees, evergreen structure and seasonal planting are considered for privacy, shade and depth. Planting must establish a setting, not merely fill the remaining ground.

Building and landscape are composed as one address.

Context

Specific to its address.

The design begins with the address. Street width, neighbouring buildings, orientation, existing vegetation, views and daylight each place specific demands on the project.

This is especially relevant along Bağdat Caddesi and its established residential neighbourhoods. New buildings must meet present-day expectations while respecting the scale, rhythm and character of the streets they join.

The address should be legible in the architecture.

The design process

From first line
to final detail

  1. 01

    Context

    Site conditions, orientation, planning constraints and the surrounding neighbourhood define the brief.

  2. 02

    Proportion

    Room relationships, circulation and structural geometry are tested together within one plan.

  3. 03

    Material

    Materials are assessed for performance, durability, texture and compatibility within the finished space.

  4. 04

    Coordination

    Architecture, structure, façade, interiors and building services are coordinated before site execution.

  5. 05

    Detail

    Critical junctions are verified through drawings, samples, mock-ups and site review before installation.

Design & execution

The drawing must survive
the building site.

Drawings alone do not secure the result. Dimensions, substrates, tolerances and material junctions must be checked during construction if the design intent is to remain intact on site.

Samples and mock-ups allow colour, texture, grain direction and adjoining finishes to be reviewed before final procurement and installation.

The completed work must retain the discipline of the drawing.

Construction & engineering

Design is completed
through construction.

Architecture depends on the coordination of structure, façade, waterproofing and building services behind the visible surfaces. At Nova, design quality is measured by how faithfully the project is engineered and executed.
