03

Decarbonization

Operational Energy

Demand is reduced at source — orientation, envelope performance and system efficiency before compensating technology.

Carbon Emissions

Lower energy intensity and cleaner electrified systems reduce the emissions associated with everyday building operation.

Embodied Impact

Structure, envelope and finishes are reviewed for the environmental cost carried in the material itself.

Resource Consumption

Systems are sized for real occupancy patterns, so nothing consumes more than the building actually requires.

Performance is engineered, verified and commissioned. Figures are published only where measured project data exists.

04

Water

Efficient Use

Low-consumption fittings and metered distribution keep demand deliberately low inside every residence.

Irrigation Strategy

Planting is selected for the local climate and irrigated by zone, matched to exposure rather than a fixed schedule.

Responsible Drainage

Permeable surfaces, attenuation and controlled discharge protect the site and the network it connects to.

Potable Demand

Where regulation and quality allow, non-potable sources serve irrigation and technical uses instead of drinking water.

06

Quality of life

Daylight

Depth of plan, ceiling height and glazing position bring usable daylight into everyday rooms.

Thermal Comfort

Radiant surfaces stay closer to room temperature, removing draughts and cold walls.

Indoor Air

Filtered fresh air is delivered continuously; low-emission finishes keep what you breathe clean.

Acoustics

Separating structures, floating floors and detailed junctions protect quiet between residences.

Wellbeing

Sustainability here is felt as comfort — a calmer, healthier, quieter way of living.

07

Ecological conservation

A site plan is a decision about how much ground a building is allowed to take. Setbacks, permeable surfaces and retained planting are drawn before the architecture is.

  • Responsible landscape design
  • Ecological awareness on site
  • Reduced environmental impact
  • Architecture and landscape as one system
08

The LEED framework

Select an impact area

Energy demand, emissions and the carbon carried in materials — assessed together, from early design through operation.

Daylight, air, thermal and acoustic comfort — the measures a resident experiences every day.

Land, water and habitat — how much a site is disturbed, and how much is given back.

LEED evaluates buildings across a comprehensive framework administered by the U.S. Green Building Council. Nova Konut develops in alignment with that framework. Certification status is stated only when a rating has actually been awarded — no Nova project is described as LEED Certified before that point.

09

Sustainability, built into the architecture.

Environmental performance is considered from the earliest design decisions through engineering, material selection, construction and long-term building performance.

Nova Konut · İnşaat Yatırım A.Ş. — designing, engineering and building residential architecture in İstanbul with environmental performance held as a design constraint, not an addition.
