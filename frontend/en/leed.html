03

Decarbonisation

Operational Energy

Building orientation, solar control, thermal insulation and airtightness are considered together to reduce energy demand. Active systems are selected to meet the calculated loads.

Greenhouse Gas Emissions

Operational emissions are assessed from energy consumption, energy sources and emission factors. The climate impact of refrigerant leakage is considered separately.

Life-Cycle Impacts

Structural, envelope and finish options are assessed on the basis of equivalent function and performance, using comparable product data and consistent life-cycle boundaries.

Peak Loads and Capacity

Equipment capacities are selected for the intended operating profile, considering heating and cooling loads, simultaneous demand and part-load efficiency.

Energy design is based on the Regulation on the Energy Performance of Buildings and the TS 825 provisions applicable to the project, supported by installation and commissioning checks.

04

Water

Efficient Use

Water-efficient fittings and cisterns are selected to suit the intended use. Metering supports consumption monitoring and the identification of unusual use.

Irrigation Strategy

Planting is selected for the local climate. Irrigation zones reflect plant water requirements, solar exposure and soil conditions, with schedules informed by rainfall and soil moisture.

Rainwater Management

Runoff is managed through permeable surfaces, storage and controlled discharge, with solutions selected to suit ground conditions, groundwater, foundations and infrastructure.

Alternative Water Use

Rainwater and greywater are considered only for uses permitted by legislation, with source-appropriate treatment, separate pipework and water-quality controls. Systems required by regulation are included in the project scope.

06

Quality of Life

Daylight

Plan depth, window positions, light transmittance and shading are considered together to provide adequate daylight, control glare and limit excessive solar gains.

Thermal Comfort

Thermal insulation, airtightness, solar control and thermal zoning are assessed alongside surface temperatures, air movement and overheating risk.

Indoor Air Quality

Ventilation rates, filtration and low-emitting products are considered together, supported by construction practices that limit moisture, dust and sources of pollution.

Acoustics

Airborne and impact sound insulation are assessed in accordance with the applicable regulations, considering separating elements, resilient layers, junctions, service penetrations and flanking transmission paths.

Occupant Comfort

Thermal comfort, acoustic performance, visual comfort and indoor air quality are addressed through project-specific design criteria and appropriate assessment methods.

07

Ecological Conservation

Site planning considers the building footprint, existing vegetation and soil, open spaces and rainwater management alongside planning requirements and infrastructure.

  • Climate-adapted planting design
  • Soil and vegetation protection during construction
  • Heat-island and runoff reduction
  • Landscape coordinated with structure, waterproofing and drainage
08

The LEED v5 Framework

Select an impact area

Operational energy, refrigerants, embodied carbon in materials and transport emissions are considered across the life cycle of the building.

Health, comfort, climate resilience, accessibility and inclusion are considered together for building occupants and surrounding communities.

Land use, water, habitats and material impacts are assessed with the aim of limiting environmental degradation and restoring ecosystems.

Developed by the U.S. Green Building Council, LEED is a voluntary green building rating system with certification administered by GBCI. The appropriate version and scope are selected for each project; certification is subject to the relevant prerequisites and point requirements. LEED assessment does not replace mandatory requirements under Turkish legislation.

09

Environmental performance through integrated design.

Energy, water, materials and indoor environmental quality criteria are considered alongside architectural and engineering decisions, construction controls, and maintenance and operational requirements.
